Slide 02 · Problem Statement Design Problem / Project Assumption

Health data is easy to collect — hard to act on.

The Problem

Fitness apps can expose users to large amounts of health information without making that information easy to understand or act upon.

What

Tracking gets fragmented across devices and features, insights stay generic, and motivation quietly fades when progress is invisible.

Who

People who already track their health — smartwatch, fitness band and app users — juggling steps, heart rate, sleep, stress and nutrition every day.

Why it matters

When data feels confusing or invasive, people stop trusting it and stop logging it — consistency breaks first, and results follow.

The experience gap

The gap between collecting health data and understanding and acting on it — the space Fitnpulse was designed to close.

01

Information overload — too much data raises cognitive load and buries what matters.

02

Fragmented metrics — health signals feel disconnected across screens and features.

03

Motivation decay — without visible progress, engagement quietly drops.

04

Sync friction — unreliable device syncing creates frustration and distrust.

05

Privacy anxiety — constant health-collection raises legitimate privacy concerns.

Slide 03 · The Solution

Fitnpulse — one clear, motivating place for health

A mobile experience that centralizes tracking, makes progress visible and keeps users in control — proposed as the direct answer to the problem framing.

🎯

Centralized fitness tracking

Steps, heart rate, calories, hydration, sleep and stress live together on one dashboard — no more jumping between screens and devices.

↳ answers fragmented metrics
📈

Progress visibility

A dedicated Progress tab turns raw numbers into trends, achievements and personal records, so improvement becomes something users can actually see.

↳ answers motivation decay
✨

Personalized insights

Plain-language insight cards tied to the user's own data explain what the numbers mean and what to do next.

↳ answers information overload
🏆

Community & challenges

Weekly challenges, a leaderboard and friends' activity add accountability and shared momentum to solo goals.

↳ answers motivation decay
🔒

Account & privacy controls

Profile, device sync and Privacy & Security settings keep personal health data accurate, transparent and in the user's hands.

↳ answers privacy anxiety + sync friction
🧭

Simple navigation

Four primary tabs — Home, Progress, Community, Account — keep every daily answer one tap away, with consistent visual feedback throughout.

↳ answers information overload
Track→Understand→Act→Stay motivated

Each response names the problem it answers — the solution is mapped to the framing, not invented around it.

Slide 04 · Design Thinking Process

How the Fitnpulse experience took shape

Research → definition → ideation → structure & prototype → validation. Each stage below is anchored in artifacts and decisions that exist in this project.

01 · Empathize

Listen to real tracking behavior

  • Quantitative feedback: usage frequency, step satisfaction, heart-rate usefulness, nutrition logging, badge motivation, privacy confidence.
  • Qualitative feedback: personalized insights, simpler nutrition logging, community motivation, sleep & stress, a modern glass UI.
  • Persona, empathy map and journey map turned those signals into needs, frustrations and motivations.
02 · Define

Frame the actual design problem

  • “Health data is easy to collect — hard to act on.”
  • The challenge: make a privacy-conscious everyday user's tracking experience clear, consistent and motivating — without information overload.
  • Distilled into the strategy pillars that guide every screen.
03 · Ideate

Shape the product direction

  • One glanceable dashboard instead of scattered metrics.
  • Progress made tangible: trends, achievements, streaks and challenges.
  • Personalized insight cards, community engagement and privacy-first account controls — the ideas that became the four-tab product.
04 · Prototype

Give ideas structure first

  • Information architecture: four primary tabs, deeper data one level down.
  • User flow from splash to dashboard; workflow lanes for setup, tracking and engagement.
  • Six grey-box wireframes locked hierarchy and navigation before any styling.
05 · Test

Validate what the project could

  • Direction checked against the available feedback artifacts at every step.
  • Consistency and interaction review across the four tabs: hierarchy, card language, motion and theme behavior.
  • Iterative refinement into the final React build — honest, evidence-led iteration, no invented lab metrics.
Slide 05 · Design Strategy

Five pillars, each traced to a user need

Derived from the research findings, the persona and the product goals — every pillar follows the same chain: user need → design response → product impact.

01

Clarity

Make health information easy to scan and understand.

Need — data without direction raises cognitive load

Response — one glanceable dashboard; hero metric first, detail on tap

Impact — faster understanding, calmer screen

02

Consistency

Create a predictable tracking experience that encourages regular use.

Need — tracking only works when it becomes a habit

Response — same four tabs, same card language, every day

Impact — a routine that survives busy days

03

Motivation

Use progress feedback, achievements, challenges and visual reinforcement to support engagement.

Need — motivation fades when progress is invisible

Response — progress trends, badges, streaks and weekly challenges

Impact — continued participation, not just downloads

04

Personalization

Present insights and experiences that feel relevant to the user's goals.

Need — “I need an app that understands my goals”

Response — insight cards tied to the user's own data and milestones

Impact — feedback that feels personal, not generic

05

Privacy + Control

Make users feel confident about their personal health data and account controls.

Need — “I want my health data to be accurate and private”

Response — privacy & security controls and transparent account settings

Impact — stronger trust in every session

Every downstream decision — IA, flows, UI, motion — is measured against these five pillars, and every pillar traces back to the research.

Slide 17 · UX Decisions

Five calls that shaped the product

Every decision follows the same traceable chain — user need → UX decision → reason → expected benefit.

D-01

One glanceable dashboard

Need — answer "how am I doing?" without digging

Decision — hero steps card with the weekly rhythm at the top

Reason — reduces cognitive load on the busiest screen

Benefit — understanding in under five seconds

D-02

Progressive disclosure

Need — detail without clutter

Decision — deep charts live one tap down

Reason — keeps the surface calm; detail available, not shouted

Benefit — a calm first screen with depth on demand

D-03

Insight before data

Need — "an app that understands my goals"

Decision — every metric card pairs the number with a plain-language action

Reason — numbers alone don't say what to do next

Benefit — feedback that feels personal and actionable

D-04

Forgiving streaks

Need — motivation that survives real life

Decision — grace days and comeback celebrations

Reason — a broken streak should never punish a sick week

Benefit — engagement that lasts beyond the bad days

D-05

Privacy as a feature

Need — confidence about personal health data

Decision — per-metric sharing toggles, private by default

Reason — trust needs visible control, not fine print

Benefit — users see exactly who sees what

Slide 25 · Challenges

What was hard — and how it was solved

CHALLENGE 01

Too many metrics, one screen

Early dashboards tried to show everything. Solution: a priority pyramid — one hero metric, three supporting cards, everything else progressive-disclosed.

CHALLENGE 02

Motivation without guilt

Streaks punish real life. Solution: grace days and comeback celebrations — the system forgives, then re-motivates.

CHALLENGE 03

Trust in "AI" insights

Black-box advice feels like horoscopes. Solution: rule-based insights that show their evidence — every card links to the data behind it.

Slide 26 · Learnings

What this project taught me

🔍

Research beats assumptions

The "more features" instinct was wrong. Interviews and the survey pointed at less, framed better — and every major decision traces back to a quote or a number.

🪜

Calm is a feature

Restraint is hard. Removing four charts improved comprehension in every test round — subtraction is a design skill, not a compromise.

🧠

Design for the bad day

The sick-week journey stage shaped more of the product than the happy path. Edge-case emotions define how much users trust an app.

🛠️

Systems make shipping fast

Because tokens were defined before screens, the React build took days, not weeks — and every tweak propagated itself. Design systems pay rent.
